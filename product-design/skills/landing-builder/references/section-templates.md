# Landing section templates

Fill-in templates per section, then the three RaceModel audience variants as
worked examples. Product claims in the examples are placeholders; replace
every number with a real, dated one.

## Templates

**Hero**

```
Headline:  <outcome the visitor wants, under 10 words>
Subline:   <for whom> + <how, in one clause>
Button:    <result of the click>
Under it:  <the objection answered: free, no card, cancel any time>
Proof:     <one checkable fact with a date>
Visual:    <the real product showing the outcome, not a stock photo>
```

**How it works** (three steps, each starts with a verb the user does)

```
1. <Open / Pick / Connect> ...
2. <See / Compare / Get> ...
3. <Decide / Share / Act> ...
```

**Outcome block** (three to six)

```
<Outcome as a result>              e.g. Know the grid before qualifying
<One sentence: how, with a number> e.g. Predictions update after every practice session.
```

**FAQ** (real questions only; answer in two sentences at most)

**Final call to action**

```
<Restated outcome>
[ <Same button as the hero> ]
<The objection it answers>
```

## RaceModel, fans

- Headline: Know the result before the lights go out
- Subline: F1 predictions for every session, with our track record in
  public.
- Button: Get this weekend's prediction
- Under it: Free. No card.
- Proof: Podium called in 14 of 22 races in 2026. See every pick.
- How it works: Open the race. See who the model expects on the podium and
  why. Check it against the result on Sunday.
- Outcomes: Know the grid before qualifying. Follow your driver's chances as
  practice unfolds. Get a note before the session starts (Pro).
- FAQ: Is it free? How accurate is it? Where does the data come from? Is
  there an iPhone app? How do I cancel Pro?
- Final: Sunday is closer than you think. [Get this weekend's prediction]
  Free. No card.

## RaceModel, teams and analysts

- Headline: Race predictions your engineers can audit
- Subline: Session-by-session finishing order with published error rates,
  backtests and methodology.
- Button: Book a 20-minute walkthrough
- Under it: We show last season's predictions against the results.
- Proof: Average error of 2.1 places per driver across 2026 (definition
  linked).
- Outcomes: Compare your own pace model against an independent one. Export
  predictions per session. Shared workspace for the team.
- FAQ: What does the model use? How is error measured? Can we get raw
  probabilities? Data retention? Seats and pricing?

## RaceModel, bookmakers and media

- Headline: Calibrated F1 probabilities, delivered by API
- Subline: Win, podium and head-to-head probabilities for every session,
  with calibration published.
- Button: Request API access
- Under it: Sandbox key within one business day.
- Proof: Calibration chart for 2026; uptime and latency figures with dates.
- Outcomes: Price markets against an independent model. Publish
  probabilities with attribution. Stable schema, versioned.
- FAQ: Licensing and attribution? Update frequency? Latency around session
  start? Responsible gambling position? Contract terms?
- Compliance: no promises of winnings; the page does not encourage betting.

## A/B hypotheses backlog (example)

1. Because five-second tests misread the current slogan, we believe an
   outcome headline for fans will increase sign-ups per visitor, without
   hurting activation.
2. Because visitors who reach the track record convert more, we believe
   moving the proof strip directly under the hero will increase sign-ups,
   without raising bounce.
3. Because the button says "Sign up", we believe "Get this weekend's
   prediction" will increase taps and sign-ups, without lowering activation.
